PW-1x Assembly Guide

Every part is in hand. This is the order to put them together, what each step should look like when it is right, and where to stop. Written for a first server build, so each risky step has a drawing. The graphics card stays in the desktop; PW-1x runs on its processor alone until a card is bought.

Part 2 · The build

Twelve steps, in this order

Steps 0 to 5 are the board on the bench with no power to it. Step 7 is the first time the supply is switched on. Allow two to three hours to step 9, then the night for step 10.

  1. Before you start

    Open bench is the board flat on its box, parts mounted on it, the supply beside it, no case. The AC cord stays out of the wall until step 7 — the board’s manual says to unplug before touching components. Wear the wrist strap clipped to the supply’s shell, or touch grounded metal before you pick anything up. Leave parts bagged until the step that uses them.

    Find these on the real board, with the sheet’s Motherboard Layout panel open. The names are the ones printed on the board:

    • Socket LGA4094 Socket SP3; memory slots DDR4_A1–H1, four each side.
    • ATXPWR1 (24-pin), ATX12V1 (8-pin), ATX12V2 (4-pin), together along the top edge.
    • Both M.2 sockets, M2_1 and M2_2. Step 3 says which.
    • PANEL1, and FAN1 — one of seven fan headers. There is no processor fan header on this board.
    • Dr. Debug, a two-digit display near TPM1. It names whatever the board gets stuck on.
    • Rear: UID1, VGA1, COM1, USB, IPMI_LAN1, LAN1, LAN2, USB-C.
    IPMI / BMCa small computer on the board that runs whenever the supply is on, even with the machine off. It serves a web page for power, screen and temperatures, and it drives the VGA port.
    Looks right when

    You can point to every item above on the real board.

    Stop if

    A label on the sheet does not match the silkscreen. The sheet covers two boards and there are revisions — trust the board.

  2. Processor into the socket

    Ground yourself first: wrist strap on the supply’s shell, or touch a grounded metal object.

    The one step where force in the wrong place is permanent. Three captive T20 screws, lettered A, B, C. The board’s manual gives one order for both directions: A → B → C. (AMD’s general SP3 documentation says loosen in reverse; both agree on the fastening order, which is the one that seats it.)

    • Loosen A, B, C. Lift the outer force frame, then the rail frame under it.
    • Take out the internal plastic cover and keep it. Now the contacts are exposed — do not touch them.
    • Hold the processor by its carrier frame; it never comes off.
    • Orientation. Find the triangle on the carrier and the one on the rail frame before you lift anything — they finish on the same corner. Photograph the closed socket first if it helps you keep track.
    • Slide the carrier into the rail frame’s guides until it clicks. Plastic into plastic, no effort. Resistance means it is 180° out — turn it round, never push.
    • Lower the rail frame, lower the force frame, tighten A → B → C. 1.58 N·m if you have a torque driver.
    Looks right when

    The carrier slid in with no resistance and clicked; all three screws turned freely and stopped at the same firmness.

    Stop if

    The carrier needs any pressure to seat — it is the wrong way round. Or a screw resists before it stops, or the frame will not sit flat: open it again and check the plastic cover came out and the carrier is fully in its guides. Never push harder.

  3. Memory, all eight sticks

    Ground yourself first: wrist strap on the supply’s shell, or touch a grounded metal object.

    Every slot gets a stick — eight channels, eight slots, and the bandwidth this machine is built on only exists with all eight filled.

    • Press the clip outward. One clip per slot, at one end; the other end is a fixed lip.
    • The notch on the gold edge is off-centre. Match it to the ridge — it only fits one way.
    • Press straight down on both ends evenly, then lock the clip by hand. Do not assume it closed itself.
    • All eight. Then run a finger along every clip: flush, and every stick at the same height.
    ECC RDIMMserver memory. ECC corrects single-bit errors instead of silently corrupting data; registered means a buffer chip lets the board run eight of them at full speed. Not interchangeable with desktop memory.
    Looks right when

    Each stick took firm even pressure, sits level with its neighbours, clip locked flush.

    Stop if

    A stick needs real force. It is the wrong way round — check the notch.

  4. The SSD into M2_2

    Ground yourself first: wrist strap on the supply’s shell, or touch a grounded metal object.

    Use M2_2

    M2_1 shares lanes with PCIE2 and the OCuLink ports, and the PE16_SEL jumper switches it off in two of its four documented positions — silently. A drive in it would just not appear. No jumper touches M2_2. Check the silkscreen, not which one is on the left.

    • Move the standoff to position D, the 8cm nut, for this 2280 drive. Peel the yellow film off the nut first. Hand-tighten it; no driver.
    • Insert the stick at about 20° until it stops. It springs up — normal.
    • Press flat and fix with the small screw. Snug; the manual says not to overtighten.
    Looks right when

    The stick lies flat and parallel to the board, screwed at its far end, chips up, in M2_2.

    Stop if

    It will not go in far enough for the screw to reach. The gold edge is not fully seated — take it out and re-insert at the angle.

  5. The cooler

    Ground yourself first: wrist strap on the supply’s shell, or touch a grounded metal object.

    • Clean the lid first. With the socket closed, wipe the metal lid with isopropyl until it is bare, dull metal. A used processor carries old paste, and new over old runs hot. Let it dry.
    • Peel the film off the cooler’s cold plate. The flat copper face that meets the processor ships with a clear or printed protective film. It is easy to miss and leaving it on is the classic way to cook a chip in minutes. Wipe the bare plate with isopropyl too.
    • Fit ARCTIC’s SP3 / TR4 bracket — PH1 screwdriver, screws up from under the mounting frame into the socket’s own holes.
    • Spread the MX-6 thin over the whole lid with the spreader or a card. The lid is far bigger than a desktop chip’s and 0.8g is one application.
    • Seat the tower with its airflow arrow pointing at the rear ports. Tighten the nuts a little at a time, crosswise, until they stop.
    • Clip both fans on and plug the lead into FAN1. If there are two leads, the second goes to FAN2.
    Looks right when

    The tower does not rock when you push its top; the arrows point at the ports; the lead is in FAN1.

    Stop if

    The heatsink lands on the memory clips or will not sit flat. Check you used the SP3 / TR4 hardware.

  6. Power supply cables

    Ground yourself first: wrist strap on the supply’s shell, or touch a grounded metal object.

    Fully modular, so only three cables go to the board. No graphics card means no PCIe cable, and the board’s GFX_12V1 stays empty. Supply switch stays at O and the AC cord stays out.

    • 24-pin → ATXPWR1. Push until the latch clicks, then tug.
    • CPU cable 1, 8-pin end → ATX12V1.
    • CPU cable 2, one 4-pin half → ATX12V2. Tie off the other half. Both 12V connectors get one.
    EPS / ATX12Vthe 12-volt cables feeding the processor’s voltage regulators directly, separate from the 24-pin. A 155W server chip with eight memory channels needs two connectors, an 8-pin and a 4-pin.
    Looks right when

    Three cables in, all latched, spare half tied off, supply beside the board with its fan in open air and nothing lying on the memory.

    Stop if

    A plug needs force or sits proud. Check the key. Never power on with a half-seated 12V connector.

  7. Monitor, keyboard, network

    • Monitor → VGA1. Not COM1 beside it, which is serial.
    • Keyboard → any rear USB.
    • Cable 1 → IPMI_LAN1, the network port that is not one of the 10GbE pair. Cable 2 → LAN1. Both to the router.
    • Nothing else yet.

    Once power is on, the speed LEDs tell you what negotiated. IPMI_LAN1: green = 1 Gbps, yellow = 100 Mbps. LAN1/LAN2: green = 10 Gbps, and a dark speed LED with a live link means 1 Gbps, not a fault.

    Looks right when

    Two network cables in two different ports, the management one away from the pair.

    Stop if

    You cannot tell the management port from the 10GbE pair. The sheet’s I/O Panel drawing numbers them — do not guess.

  8. First power

    • Plug the AC cord into the wall — first time — and switch the supply to I. Fans may twitch. Wait two minutes while the management controller boots. Black screen is normal.
    • Press power. Three ways: the management page; a screwdriver tip for one second across PWRBTN# and the GND beside it on PANEL1; or a pigtail.
    • Wait again. First boot with 128GB takes two to four minutes to train memory before anything appears. Not a failure until five have passed.
    POSTthe board’s own checks before any operating system. The first POST after new memory is slow; later ones are much faster.

    Nothing after five minutes

    Read Dr. Debug first — the two-digit code names the stage it stopped at. Photograph it, then look it up. There will be no beeps; the board has a SPEAKER1 header but no speaker was bought.

    Then switch off and check, each fully: both 12V connectors seated; the 24-pin latched; all eight memory clips locked; the three socket screws snug; the monitor on VGA1 and that input selected.

  9. First visit to the BIOS

    Press Del at the splash.

    • Processor: EPYC 7452, 32 cores.
    • Memory: 131072 MB at 3200. If it reads 65536 or 98304, sticks are not seated — power off and check every clip.
    • Storage: the Kingston listed. If not, check it is in M2_2.
    • Note the management IP under Server Mgmt → BMC Network, or read it off the router’s lease table.
    • Set boot to UEFI and set the date. Change nothing else — NPS stays 1, SMT on.

  10. Log in to the management page

    • From the desktop, browse to the management IP. Default login admin / admin; it forces a change. Put the new password in the sops file, not here.
    • Open Sensors and write down idle VRM, DIMM and processor temperatures. This is the page read again under load.
    • Check LAN1’s speed LED while you are here.

    From now on this page is the power button, the screen and the thermometer — no monitor needed at the bench.

  11. memtest86+ overnight

    The step with a deadline: 13 October 2026 at the latest, earlier if the invoice shows an earlier delivery. A faulty stick found inside the window is a vendor-paid return. Found later it is a warranty claim needing a matched replacement.

    • Write memtest86+ to the USB stick from memtest.org (or its GitHub releases). Take the free memtest86+, not the commercial MemTest86. Windows: the USB installer. Linux/macOS: dd the .img to the base device, not a partition.
    • Boot it from the board’s boot menu (usually F11); pick the UEFI entry if offered.
    • All passes, all night, all eight sticks.
    • Morning: zero errors over two or more passes is a pass. Photograph the screen with the count and date.
    • Any error: test one stick at a time in DDR4_A1 until you find it, then a defective return.
    Looks right when

    Errors 0, two or more passes, 128GB at the top of the screen.

    Stop if

    memtest shows less than 128GB — a stick is not detected. Back to step 8.

  12. Record, then hand over

    Into notes/: photos of the finished bench; the management IP; idle temperatures; the memtest result and its date. The new IPMI password goes in the sops file. Then the OS install starts, from the plan.

SymptomMost likelyDo this

Nothing turns on at allLooking for a power button that does not existShort PWRBTN# and GND on PANEL1, or use the management page. The rear button is UID1, a light.
